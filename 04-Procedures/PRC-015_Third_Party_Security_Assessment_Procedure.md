# PRC-015 Third-Party Security Assessment Procedure

**Procedure ID:** PRC-015

**Version:** 1.0

**Owner:** Third-Party Risk Manager

**Approved By:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

**Effective Date:** July 2026

**Review Cycle:** Annual

**Classification:** Internal

**Supporting Documents:**

- GOV-029 Enterprise Third-Party Risk Management Policy
- STD-001 Enterprise Risk Management Standard
- STD-015 Enterprise Information Classification and Handling Standard
- STD-016 Enterprise Third-Party Risk Management Standard
- STD-017 Enterprise Supplier Security Standard

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this procedure is to establish a standardized process for assessing, approving, monitoring, and documenting third-party security risks before and throughout the lifecycle of supplier relationships.

This procedure ensures third parties are evaluated using a consistent, risk-based approach that protects enterprise information assets, supports regulatory compliance, and reduces supply chain cybersecurity risk.

---

# 2. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- Cloud service providers.
- Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) providers.
- Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS) providers.
- Managed Service Providers (MSPs).
- Managed Security Service Providers (MSSPs).
- Professional service vendors.
- Consultants.
- Contractors.
- Payment processors.
- Business partners.
- Data processors.
- Suppliers with access to enterprise information or systems.

This procedure applies before contract execution and throughout the third-party relationship.

---

# 3. Procedure Objectives

The objectives of this procedure are to:

- Assess third-party cybersecurity risk.
- Protect enterprise information assets.
- Ensure regulatory compliance.
- Standardize vendor security assessments.
- Support informed risk-based decisions.
- Monitor supplier security posture.
- Reduce supply chain cyber risk.
- Maintain complete assessment documentation.

---

# 4. Prerequisites

Before initiating a third-party security assessment, the following shall be completed:

- Vendor request submitted.
- Business owner identified.
- Procurement request initiated.
- Service description available.
- Data classification identified.
- Information flow documented.
- Vendor contact information obtained.
- Initial business impact identified.
- Risk assessment template available.

---

# 5. Roles and Responsibilities

## Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) Team

The TPRM Team shall:

- Coordinate third-party security assessments.
- Perform vendor risk evaluations.
- Review assessment documentation.
- Maintain the third-party risk register.
- Track remediation activities.
- Produce third-party risk reports.

## Business Owner

The Business Owner shall:

- Initiate the vendor request.
- Define the business need.
- Identify the data being shared.
- Participate in vendor risk discussions.
- Accept business responsibility for the vendor relationship.
- Support remediation activities.

## Procurement Team

The Procurement Team shall:

- Verify procurement requirements.
- Coordinate vendor onboarding.
- Ensure required assessments are completed before contract execution.
- Maintain procurement documentation.
- Coordinate contract renewals.

## Information Security Team

The Information Security Team shall:

- Perform cybersecurity due diligence.
- Review vendor security controls.
- Validate assessment results.
- Recommend remediation activities.
- Approve security exceptions.
- Monitor vendor security risks.

## Legal and Compliance

Legal and Compliance shall:

- Review contractual security requirements.
- Validate regulatory obligations.
- Review privacy requirements.
- Coordinate contract language.
- Review security addendums.
- Support regulatory compliance.

---

# 6. Vendor Intake and Classification

Every third-party engagement shall begin with a formal vendor intake process.

Vendor intake activities include:

- Identify the vendor.
- Identify the requested service.
- Identify the Business Owner.
- Identify business justification.
- Identify systems affected.
- Identify data classifications.
- Determine whether customer information will be processed.
- Determine whether regulated data will be processed.
- Identify cloud-hosted services where applicable.
- Register the vendor in the Third-Party Risk Management platform.

Following intake, vendors shall be classified according to enterprise risk criteria.

Vendor classifications include:

- Critical
- High
- Medium
- Low

Classification shall determine the level of security assessment required.

---

# 7. Inherent Risk Assessment

An inherent risk assessment shall be completed before security due diligence begins.

Assessment criteria include:

- Data sensitivity.
- Business criticality.
- Network connectivity.
- Internet exposure.
- Privileged access requirements.
- Regulatory obligations.
- Geographic location.
- Fourth-party dependencies.
- Financial impact.
- Operational impact.
- Reputational impact.

The inherent risk rating shall be documented before vendor approval.

---

# 8. Security Due Diligence

Security due diligence shall be conducted for all vendors classified as Medium, High, or Critical risk.

Due diligence activities include:

- Review security documentation.
- Review information security policies.
- Review access control practices.
- Review identity and access management controls.
- Review vulnerability management practices.
- Review patch management processes.
- Review security monitoring capabilities.
- Review incident response capabilities.
- Review disaster recovery capabilities.
- Review business continuity capabilities.
- Review regulatory certifications.
- Review independent audit reports.

Security due diligence shall be completed before contract execution unless an approved exception exists.

---

# 9. Security Questionnaire Review

Applicable vendors shall complete the approved enterprise security questionnaire.

The questionnaire shall evaluate:

- Governance.
- Risk management.
- Identity and Access Management (IAM).
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Encryption.
- Logging and monitoring.
- Vulnerability management.
- Patch management.
- Secure software development.
- Cloud security.
- Privacy controls.
- Incident response.
- Business continuity.
- Disaster recovery.
- Regulatory compliance.

Responses shall be reviewed by the Information Security Team.

---

# 10. Evidence Validation

Security assertions provided by vendors shall be validated using supporting evidence where appropriate.

Acceptable evidence may include:

- ISO/IEC 27001 certification.
- SOC 2 Type II report.
- PCI DSS Attestation of Compliance (AOC).
- Penetration testing reports.
- Vulnerability assessment reports.
- Independent security audit reports.
- Cyber insurance documentation.
- Security awareness training records.
- Incident response plans.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery plans.
- Security architecture documentation.
- Data flow diagrams.

Evidence shall be reviewed for completeness, relevance, authenticity, and currency before assessment approval.
