**Document ID:** STD-021

**Version:** 1.0

**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

**Approved By:** Executive Management

**Effective Date:** July 2026

**Review Cycle:** Annual

**Classification:** Internal

**Supporting Policies:**

- GOV-024 Enterprise Security Architecture Policy
- GOV-026 Enterprise Information Security Policy
- GOV-031 Enterprise Identity and Access Management Policy
- GOV-040 Enterprise Incident Management Policy
- GOV-049 Enterprise Zero Trust Architecture Policy

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Security Awareness and Training Standard is to establish mandatory requirements for developing, delivering, and maintaining security awareness and role-based cybersecurity training across the organization.

This standard ensures that personnel understand their cybersecurity responsibilities, recognize cyber threats, protect organizational information, and respond appropriately to security incidents, thereby reducing organizational risk and strengthening cyber resilience.

---

# 2. Scope

This standard applies to:

- Employees.
- Contractors.
- Temporary workers.
- Interns.
- Consultants.
- Third-party personnel with access to organizational systems.
- Executive leadership.
- Board members where applicable.
- Information Technology personnel.
- Information Security personnel.
- Privileged users.
- Developers.
- Cloud administrators.

This standard applies to all individuals who access organizational information systems or process organizational information.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this standard are to:

- Improve cybersecurity awareness.
- Reduce human-related cybersecurity risks.
- Strengthen phishing resistance.
- Promote secure handling of organizational information.
- Support regulatory compliance.
- Improve incident reporting.
- Reinforce Zero Trust principles.
- Reduce insider threats.
- Promote secure remote work practices.
- Establish a culture of cybersecurity.

---

# 4. Standard Statement

The organization shall establish, maintain, and continuously improve a Security Awareness and Training Program that provides personnel with the knowledge and skills required to protect organizational information and technology assets.

Security awareness activities shall be risk-based, role-specific where appropriate, regularly updated, and measured for effectiveness.

Training shall be mandatory for all personnel and completed within established organizational timeframes.

---

# 5. Security Awareness Program Governance

The organization shall establish governance processes to ensure the Security Awareness and Training Program is effectively managed, monitored, and continuously improved.

Program governance requirements include:

- Documented Security Awareness Program.
- Defined program ownership.
- Executive oversight.
- Integration with Enterprise Risk Management (ERM).
- Annual review of awareness content.
- Periodic program effectiveness assessments.
- Compliance with legal and regulatory requirements.
- Continuous improvement of awareness materials.

The Security Awareness Program shall be reviewed at least annually or following significant business, regulatory, technology, or threat landscape changes.

---

# 6. Mandatory Security Awareness Training

All personnel shall complete mandatory security awareness training upon hire and annually thereafter.

Mandatory training shall include:

- Information security policies.
- Acceptable Use Policy.
- Password security.
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Data classification.
- Data Loss Prevention (DLP).
- Email security.
- Safe internet browsing.
- Mobile device security.
- Cloud security awareness.
- Incident reporting procedures.
- Physical security awareness.

Personnel who fail to complete mandatory training within established deadlines may have access to enterprise systems restricted until training is completed.

---

# 7. Role-Based Security Training

Personnel with elevated responsibilities shall receive additional role-based cybersecurity training.

Role-based training shall be provided for:

- Executive leadership.
- Information Security personnel.
- Information Technology administrators.
- System administrators.
- Privileged Access Management (PAM) administrators.
- Developers.
- DevSecOps engineers.
- Cloud administrators.
- Security Operations Center (SOC) analysts.
- Help Desk personnel.
- Third-Party Risk Management personnel.

Role-based training shall address risks specific to assigned responsibilities.

---

# 8. Phishing Simulation Program

The organization shall conduct periodic phishing simulation exercises to evaluate personnel awareness and strengthen resistance to phishing attacks.

Phishing simulation requirements include:

- Organization-wide phishing campaigns.
- Targeted campaigns for high-risk personnel.
- Measurement of phishing susceptibility.
- Immediate user feedback following simulation.
- Additional training for personnel who fail simulations.
- Executive reporting of campaign results.
- Tracking of improvement trends.
- Protection of individual privacy during reporting.

Simulation results shall be used to improve awareness activities and reduce phishing-related risks.

---

# 9. Social Engineering Awareness

Personnel shall receive training on recognizing and responding to social engineering attacks.

Training topics include:

- Phishing.
- Spear phishing.
- Whaling.
- Business Email Compromise (BEC).
- Vishing (voice phishing).
- Smishing (SMS phishing).
- Tailgating.
- Impersonation attacks.
- Pretexting.
- Physical security awareness.
- Credential harvesting attacks.

Personnel shall report suspected social engineering attempts immediately.

---

# 10. Secure Password and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) Awareness

Personnel shall receive training on secure authentication practices.

Training shall include:

- Creation of strong passphrases.
- Password manager usage.
- Protection of authentication credentials.
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) requirements.
- Risks of password reuse.
- Credential phishing awareness.
- Secure password reset procedures.
- Reporting suspected credential compromise.
- Secure authentication for remote work.
- Enterprise Identity and Access Management (IAM) requirements.

Personnel shall never share passwords or authentication tokens with other individuals.
