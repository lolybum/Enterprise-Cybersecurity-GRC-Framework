# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Third-Party Risk Management Standard is to establish mandatory security, governance, and operational requirements for identifying, assessing, onboarding, monitoring, and offboarding third-party service providers that access organizational information, systems, or facilities.

This standard ensures third-party relationships are managed using a consistent risk-based approach that protects organizational assets, supports regulatory compliance, and reduces supply chain cybersecurity risk.

---

# 2. Scope

This standard applies to:

- Vendors.
- Suppliers.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Managed Service Providers (MSPs).
- Managed Security Service Providers (MSSPs).
- Cloud Service Providers.
- Software vendors.
- Payment processors.
- Outsourcing providers.
- Professional service firms.
- Fourth-party providers where applicable.

This standard applies to all third parties that create, process, store, transmit, or have access to organizational information or technology assets.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this standard are to:

- Reduce third-party cyber risk.
- Standardize vendor security assessments.
- Strengthen supply chain security.
- Protect organizational information.
- Improve regulatory compliance.
- Support Zero Trust Architecture.
- Improve vendor oversight.
- Reduce operational risk.
- Improve cyber resilience.
- Support continuous vendor monitoring.

---

# 4. Standard Statement

The organization shall establish and maintain a risk-based Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) program that evaluates cybersecurity, privacy, operational, legal, and regulatory risks throughout the lifecycle of third-party relationships.

Third parties shall undergo security assessment before onboarding, continuous monitoring throughout the engagement, and secure offboarding upon contract termination.

Security requirements shall be incorporated into procurement, contracting, vendor management, and supplier oversight activities.

---

# 5. Third-Party Security Governance

The organization shall establish governance processes to ensure third-party risks are identified, assessed, managed, monitored, and reviewed throughout the vendor lifecycle.

Third-party security governance requirements include:

- Documented Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) procedures.
- Defined governance roles and responsibilities.
- Executive oversight of third-party cyber risk.
- Integration with Enterprise Risk Management (ERM).
- Risk-based vendor oversight.
- Periodic governance reviews.
- Alignment with procurement processes.
- Continuous improvement of the TPRM program.

Third-party governance activities shall be reviewed at least annually or following significant business, regulatory, or technology changes.

---

# 6. Vendor Due Diligence

Security due diligence shall be completed before entering into contracts with third parties.

Due diligence activities include:

- Security capability assessments.
- Review of regulatory compliance.
- Evaluation of information security policies.
- Review of cybersecurity certifications (e.g., ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2).
- Assessment of incident response capabilities.
- Review of business continuity and disaster recovery capabilities.
- Evaluation of data protection controls.
- Assessment of financial stability where appropriate.
- Review of subcontractor management practices.
- Verification of legal and regulatory obligations.

Due diligence results shall be documented and approved before onboarding.

---

# 7. Vendor Risk Classification

Third parties shall be classified according to the level of risk they present to the organization.

Risk classification criteria include:

- Sensitivity of information accessed.
- Volume of organizational data processed.
- Access to production systems.
- Internet-facing services.
- Regulatory obligations.
- Business criticality.
- Dependency on vendor services.
- Geographic location.
- Use of subcontractors.
- Results of vendor security assessments.

Vendor risk ratings shall be documented as:

- Critical
- High
- Medium
- Low

Risk classifications shall be reviewed annually or when significant changes occur.

---

# 8. Security Questionnaires

Appropriate security questionnaires shall be completed before onboarding high-risk and critical vendors.

Questionnaires shall evaluate:

- Information security governance.
- Identity and Access Management (IAM).
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Network security.
- Endpoint security.
- Vulnerability management.
- Patch management.
- Encryption practices.
- Logging and monitoring.
- Incident response.
- Backup and recovery.
- Secure software development practices.
- Third-party risk management.
- Privacy and regulatory compliance.

Responses shall be reviewed by the Information Security Team and retained as part of the vendor risk assessment record.

---

# 9. Contractual Security Requirements

Contracts with third parties shall include appropriate cybersecurity and information security requirements.

Security contract provisions shall address:

- Information security obligations.
- Confidentiality requirements.
- Data ownership.
- Privacy obligations.
- Encryption requirements.
- Access control requirements.
- Incident notification timelines.
- Security audit rights.
- Compliance with applicable laws and regulations.
- Vulnerability remediation expectations.
- Secure data disposal upon contract termination.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery obligations.
- Cybersecurity insurance requirements where applicable.

Legal counsel shall review security clauses before contract execution.

---

# 10. Third-Party Onboarding

Third parties shall complete an approved onboarding process before being granted access to organizational systems, facilities, or information.

Onboarding requirements include:

- Completion of due diligence activities.
- Vendor risk assessment approval.
- Signed contractual agreements.
- Defined business owner.
- Access approval.
- Identity verification.
- Least privilege access assignment.
- Security awareness requirements where applicable.
- Documentation of approved services.
- Entry into the Enterprise Vendor Inventory.

Third-party access shall not be granted until all onboarding requirements have been completed and approved.
