# GOV-012 Enterprise Identity and Access Management (IAM) Policy

**Document ID:** GOV-012  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)  
**Approved By:** Executive Management  
**Effective Date:** July 2026  
**Review Cycle:** Annual

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish enterprise requirements for managing digital identities and controlling access to organizational information systems throughout the identity lifecycle. Effective Identity and Access Management (IAM) ensures that only authorized individuals receive appropriate access to enterprise resources while supporting regulatory compliance, reducing cybersecurity risk, and protecting sensitive information.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees
- Contractors
- Third-party service providers
- Vendors
- Temporary personnel
- Interns
- Business partners
- Cloud services
- On-premises infrastructure
- Enterprise applications
- Network devices
- Databases
- Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) platforms
- Identity providers
- All organizational information assets

---

# 3. Objectives

The organization shall:

- Protect digital identities.
- Ensure authorized access to enterprise resources.
- Enforce least privilege.
- Support Zero Trust principles.
- Improve accountability.
- Reduce insider threats.
- Protect sensitive information.
- Meet legal and regulatory requirements.
- Strengthen enterprise cybersecurity resilience.

---

# 4. IAM Policy Statement

The organization shall implement a centralized Identity and Access Management (IAM) program to govern user identities, authentication, authorization, provisioning, access reviews, and account lifecycle management.

All user access shall be approved, monitored, periodically reviewed, and promptly removed when no longer required.

---

# 5. Identity Lifecycle Management

Identity lifecycle management shall include:

- Identity creation
- Identity verification
- Account provisioning
- Role assignment
- Access modification
- Periodic access review
- Account suspension
- Account deprovisioning
- Secure identity archival
- Identity record retention

---

# 6. User Provisioning

User provisioning shall:

- Require documented business justification.
- Be approved by the appropriate manager.
- Follow role-based access assignments.
- Be completed using standardized provisioning procedures.
- Assign unique user identities.
- Grant only approved access rights.
- Be recorded within the enterprise identity management system.

---

# 7. User Deprovisioning

User accounts shall be removed or modified:

- Immediately upon employee termination.
- Following contractor engagement completion.
- Following role changes.
- Following departmental transfers.
- Following extended periods of inactivity.
- Following security incidents when required.
- According to established identity lifecycle procedures.

Privileged access shall be revoked before standard access where applicable.

---

# 8. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

The organization shall implement Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) by:

- Assigning permissions based on job responsibilities.
- Enforcing segregation of duties (SoD).
- Minimizing excessive privileges.
- Reviewing role definitions periodically.
- Maintaining standardized enterprise roles.
- Preventing privilege accumulation.
- Supporting least privilege principles.

---

# 9. Authentication Requirements

Authentication controls shall include:

- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Strong password requirements
- Password manager usage where approved
- Single Sign-On (SSO) where applicable
- Adaptive authentication
- Account lockout protections
- Secure password storage
- Session timeout controls
- Continuous authentication where supported

Administrative accounts shall require MFA at all times.

---

# 10. Federation and Single Sign-On (SSO)

Federated identity services shall:

- Support trusted identity providers.
- Protect authentication tokens.
- Use secure federation protocols.
- Support Single Sign-On (SSO).
- Reduce password reuse.
- Improve user experience.
- Maintain centralized identity governance.

---

# 11. Access Reviews and Recertification

User access shall be reviewed periodically to ensure continued business need.

Access reviews shall:

- Be conducted at least quarterly for privileged accounts.
- Be conducted at least annually for all user accounts.
- Verify role appropriateness.
- Remove unnecessary permissions.
- Validate segregation of duties.
- Document review results.
- Retain review records for audit purposes.

---

# 12. Third-Party Identity Management

Third-party users shall:

- Receive formally approved accounts.
- Be assigned unique identities.
- Be granted only required access.
- Use Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Be subject to periodic access reviews.
- Have defined account expiration dates.
- Be deprovisioned immediately upon contract termination.

Third-party access shall comply with the organization's vendor risk management requirements.

# 14. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy shall:

- Be documented.
- Include business justification.
- Be approved by Executive Management.
- Include compensating security controls.
- Include an expiration date.
- Be reviewed periodically.

Approved exceptions shall be maintained within the enterprise exception register.

# 19. Definitions

**Identity** – A unique digital representation of an individual, system, application, or service within the enterprise.

**Identity and Access Management (IAM)** – The framework of policies, processes, and technologies used to manage digital identities and control access to enterprise resources.

**Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)** – An access control model that grants permissions based on organizational job roles.

**Authentication** – The process of verifying the identity of a user, device, or service before granting access.

**Authorization** – The process of determining what authenticated users are permitted to access or perform.
