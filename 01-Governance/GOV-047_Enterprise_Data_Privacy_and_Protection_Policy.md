# GOV-047 Enterprise Data Privacy and Protection Policy

**Document ID:** GOV-047

**Version:** 1.0

**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

**Approved By:** Executive Management

**Effective Date:** July 2026

**Review Cycle:** Annual

**Classification:** Internal

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Data Privacy and Protection Policy is to establish governance requirements for the collection, processing, storage, sharing, retention, protection, and disposal of personal information and other regulated data throughout its lifecycle.

This policy establishes a standardized enterprise privacy governance framework that protects the rights of individuals, supports lawful and transparent data processing, reduces privacy-related risks, and ensures compliance with applicable privacy laws, regulations, contractual obligations, and organizational standards.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Temporary workers.
- Interns.
- Executive leadership.
- Information Technology personnel.
- Information Security personnel.
- Human Resources personnel.
- Legal and Compliance personnel.
- Third-party vendors.
- Managed service providers.
- Business partners processing organizational data.
- Cloud service providers.

This policy applies to all organizational activities involving personal information and regulated data, including:

- Collection.
- Recording.
- Organization.
- Storage.
- Use.
- Processing.
- Sharing.
- Disclosure.
- Transfer.
- Archiving.
- Retention.
- Disposal.

The policy applies to personal data belonging to employees, customers, applicants, contractors, suppliers, business partners, and any other identifiable individuals whose information is processed by the organization.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Establish enterprise privacy governance.
- Protect personal information.
- Support lawful and transparent processing.
- Reduce privacy risks.
- Strengthen data protection controls.
- Enable Privacy by Design and Privacy by Default.
- Support regulatory compliance.
- Protect individual privacy rights.
- Improve accountability for personal data.
- Promote responsible data handling throughout the organization.

---

# 4. Policy Statement

The organization shall maintain a formal Enterprise Data Privacy and Protection Program governing the lawful, fair, transparent, secure, and accountable processing of personal information.

Personal information shall be processed only for authorized business purposes, protected using appropriate administrative, technical, and physical safeguards, and retained only as long as necessary to satisfy legal, regulatory, contractual, and business requirements.

Privacy requirements shall be integrated into business processes, technology implementations, system development, vendor management, and organizational decision-making through Privacy by Design and Privacy by Default principles.

The organization shall continuously monitor privacy risks, maintain appropriate governance, and comply with applicable privacy laws and regulations in every jurisdiction where it operates.

---

# 5. Privacy Governance

The organization shall maintain a formal privacy governance framework to oversee the protection of personal information and compliance with applicable privacy laws and regulations.

Privacy governance shall include:

- Executive sponsorship.
- Defined governance roles and responsibilities.
- Enterprise privacy standards.
- Risk-based privacy management.
- Regulatory compliance oversight.
- Privacy awareness and training.
- Executive reporting.
- Periodic governance reviews.
- Privacy performance monitoring.
- Continuous improvement initiatives.

Privacy governance shall align with Enterprise Risk Management, Information Security, Legal, Human Resources, and Compliance functions.

---

# 6. Personal Data Classification

Personal information shall be classified according to its sensitivity, regulatory requirements, and business impact.

Personal data categories may include:

- Public personal information.
- Internal personal information.
- Confidential personal information.
- Restricted personal information.
- Sensitive personal information.
- Financial information.
- Health information.
- Biometric information.
- Children's information.
- Government-issued identification information.

Security controls shall be implemented based on the assigned classification level.

---

# 7. Lawful Basis for Processing

Personal information shall be processed only when there is a valid legal or regulatory basis.

Lawful bases for processing may include:

- Individual consent.
- Performance of a contract.
- Compliance with legal obligations.
- Protection of vital interests.
- Legitimate business interests.
- Public interest where applicable.
- Employment-related obligations.
- Regulatory requirements.
- Fraud prevention.
- Information security purposes.

The lawful basis for processing shall be documented where required by applicable law.

---

# 8. Privacy by Design and Privacy by Default

Privacy requirements shall be integrated into business processes, systems, and technologies from the earliest stages of planning through retirement.

Privacy by Design principles include:

- Proactive privacy protection.
- Privacy as the default setting.
- Privacy embedded into system design.
- Full lifecycle protection.
- Data minimization.
- Strong security safeguards.
- Transparency.
- User-centric privacy controls.
- Continuous privacy monitoring.
- Regulatory compliance.

Privacy reviews shall be completed for new systems, services, and significant changes affecting personal information.

---

# 9. Data Subject Rights

The organization shall support applicable data subject rights in accordance with relevant privacy laws and regulations.

Supported rights may include:

- Right to access.
- Right to correction.
- Right to deletion.
- Right to restrict processing.
- Right to data portability.
- Right to object to processing.
- Right to withdraw consent.
- Right to opt out where applicable.
- Right to appeal privacy decisions.
- Right to lodge complaints with regulatory authorities where applicable.

Requests shall be processed within applicable legal timeframes.

---

# 10. Consent Management

Where consent is required, the organization shall obtain, document, manage, and maintain valid consent.

Consent management requirements include:

- Freely given consent.
- Specific consent.
- Informed consent.
- Unambiguous consent.
- Consent documentation.
- Consent withdrawal mechanisms.
- Consent record retention.
- Periodic consent review.
- Age verification where applicable.
- Regulatory compliance.

Consent records shall be protected and maintained as evidence of lawful processing.

---

# 11. Data Minimization

The organization shall collect, process, and retain only the minimum amount of personal information necessary to achieve legitimate business purposes.

Data minimization requirements include:

- Collection of only necessary personal information.
- Purpose limitation.
- Limited access based on business need.
- Periodic review of collected information.
- Removal of unnecessary personal information.
- Secure anonymization where appropriate.
- Secure pseudonymization where appropriate.
- Minimization during testing and development.
- Compliance with retention schedules.
- Regulatory compliance.

Business processes shall be designed to minimize unnecessary collection and processing of personal information.

---

# 12. Cross-Border Data Transfers

Transfers of personal information across national borders shall comply with applicable legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements.

Cross-border transfer requirements include:

- Legal review.
- Privacy risk assessment.
- Approved transfer mechanisms.
- Data Processing Agreements (DPAs).
- Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) where applicable.
- Vendor due diligence.
- Encryption during transmission.
- Secure storage.
- Documentation of transfers.
- Periodic compliance reviews.

Cross-border transfers shall be approved before personal information is transmitted internationally.

---

# 13. Data Sharing

Personal information shall be shared only for authorized business purposes and with approved recipients.

Data sharing requirements include:

- Business justification.
- Least privilege access.
- Data sharing agreements.
- Confidentiality obligations.
- Third-party security assessments.
- Data minimization.
- Secure transmission.
- Logging of data sharing activities.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Periodic review of sharing arrangements.

Unauthorized disclosure of personal information is prohibited.

---

# 14. Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs)

Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs) shall be conducted for new systems, projects, services, or significant changes involving personal information.

PIAs shall include:

- Description of processing activities.
- Identification of personal information processed.
- Privacy risk assessment.
- Security control assessment.
- Third-party involvement.
- Legal and regulatory review.
- Data retention review.
- Mitigation recommendations.
- Executive approval where required.
- Documentation of assessment results.

PIAs shall be completed before implementation of high-risk processing activities.

---

# 15. Data Breach Notification

Privacy-related data breaches shall be managed in accordance with enterprise incident management and applicable privacy laws.

Data breach notification requirements include:

- Immediate incident reporting.
- Breach investigation.
- Containment activities.
- Risk assessment.
- Regulatory notification where required.
- Notification of affected individuals where required.
- Documentation of breach activities.
- Evidence preservation.
- Corrective action implementation.
- Post-incident review.

Notification timelines shall comply with applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

---

# 16. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Management

Executive Management shall:

- Approve the Enterprise Data Privacy and Protection Program.
- Allocate appropriate resources.
- Review enterprise privacy risks.
- Support continuous improvement initiatives.

## Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The CISO shall:

- Own this policy.
- Oversee privacy security controls.
- Coordinate with Legal and Compliance.
- Review privacy metrics.
- Report significant privacy risks to Executive Management.

## Privacy Officer / Data Protection Officer (DPO)

The Privacy Officer or Data Protection Officer (DPO), where appointed, shall:

- Oversee privacy governance.
- Monitor compliance with privacy laws.
- Review Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs).
- Coordinate responses to data subject requests.
- Support regulatory communications.
- Recommend privacy improvements.

## Information Security Team

The Information Security Team shall:

- Implement technical privacy safeguards.
- Protect personal information.
- Monitor privacy security controls.
- Investigate privacy-related security incidents.
- Support privacy risk assessments.
- Maintain security documentation.

## Business Owners

Business Owners shall:

- Ensure lawful processing of personal information.
- Participate in Privacy Impact Assessments.
- Protect personal information within their business processes.
- Support compliance activities.
- Report privacy concerns promptly.

## Employees

Employees shall:

- Protect personal information.
- Follow enterprise privacy requirements.
- Report suspected privacy incidents immediately.
- Complete required privacy awareness training.
- Use personal information only for authorized business purposes.

# 17. Compliance

Compliance with this policy shall be verified through:

- Internal audits.
- External audits.
- Privacy governance assessments.
- Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) reviews.
- Regulatory compliance assessments.
- Privacy control testing.
- Data protection audits.
- Continuous monitoring.
- Third-party privacy assessments.

- Mandatory privacy awareness training.

---

# 18. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy shall:

- Be formally documented.
- Include business justification.
- Include a documented privacy and security risk assessment.
- Identify compensating controls.
- Be approved by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) and the Privacy Officer or Data Protection Officer (DPO), where applicable.
- Include an expiration date.
- Be reviewed annually.

Approved exceptions shall be maintained within the Enterprise Exception Register.

---

- Employee awareness feedback.

Program effectiveness shall be reviewed annually.

# 22. Definitions

**Personal Information (Personal Data)** – Information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, as defined by applicable privacy laws.

**Sensitive Personal Information** – Personal information requiring enhanced protection due to legal, regulatory, or business requirements, including financial, health, biometric, and government-issued identification information.

**Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA)** – A documented process used to identify and mitigate privacy risks associated with new or changed systems, services, or business processes.

**Data Subject** – An individual whose personal information is collected, processed, stored, or otherwise handled by the organization.

**Data Controller** – The entity that determines the purposes and means of processing personal information, where applicable under relevant privacy laws.

**Data Processor** – An entity that processes personal information on behalf of a Data Controller, where applicable under relevant privacy laws.
