# GOV-027 Enterprise Email Security Policy

**Document ID:** GOV-027

**Version:** 1.0

**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

**Approved By:** Executive Management

**Effective Date:** July 2026

**Review Cycle:** Annual

**Classification:** Internal

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Email Security Policy is to establish enterprise-wide requirements for securing email systems, protecting organizational communications, preventing email-based cyber threats, and ensuring the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of electronic messaging services.

This policy supports secure business communications while reducing the risks associated with phishing, business email compromise (BEC), malware, ransomware, spoofing, spam, and unauthorized disclosure of sensitive information.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Corporate email systems.
- Cloud-based email platforms.
- On-premises email servers.
- Email gateways.
- Mobile email clients.
- Webmail access.
- Shared mailboxes.
- Distribution lists.
- Service accounts using email.
- Third-party email integrations.
- All employees, contractors, consultants, temporary workers, interns, vendors, and third parties authorized to use organizational email systems.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Protect enterprise email communications.
- Prevent phishing attacks.
- Reduce business email compromise (BEC).
- Block malicious email attachments.
- Prevent email spoofing.
- Secure sensitive information transmitted through email.
- Improve email threat detection.
- Support regulatory compliance.
- Strengthen organizational cyber resilience.

---

# 4. Policy Statement

Enterprise email services shall be protected using layered security controls designed to detect, prevent, and respond to email-based cyber threats.

Email systems shall be centrally managed, continuously monitored, and configured using approved enterprise security standards.

Only authorized users and approved systems shall be permitted to send or receive organizational email.

---

# 5. Email Security Controls

Enterprise email systems shall implement layered security controls to protect against unauthorized access, malicious content, and data loss.

Minimum email security controls include:

- Secure email gateways.
- Anti-phishing protection.
- Anti-spam filtering.
- Malware scanning.
- URL protection.
- Attachment sandboxing.
- Email authentication.
- Data Loss Prevention (DLP).
- Email encryption.
- Security event logging.

Email security controls shall be centrally managed and reviewed regularly.

---

# 6. Anti-Phishing Protection

The organization shall implement technical and administrative controls to reduce phishing attacks.

Anti-phishing measures include:

- URL reputation analysis.
- Real-time URL rewriting.
- Attachment sandboxing.
- Display name protection.
- External sender identification.
- Machine learning threat detection.
- User phishing awareness training.
- Simulated phishing exercises.
- Email warning banners.
- Threat intelligence integration.

Suspected phishing emails shall be reported immediately to the Information Security Team.

---

# 7. Anti-Spam Protection

Enterprise email systems shall use approved anti-spam technologies to reduce unwanted and malicious email.

Spam protection shall include:

- Reputation filtering.
- Blacklist and allowlist management.
- Content filtering.
- Rate limiting.
- Domain reputation analysis.
- Sender validation.
- Real-time spam detection.
- Automated quarantine.
- Continuous monitoring.
- Periodic tuning of spam detection rules.

Users shall not disable spam protection mechanisms.

---

# 8. Malware Protection

Inbound and outbound email shall be scanned for malicious content.

Malware protection shall include:

- Virus scanning.
- Ransomware detection.
- Trojan detection.
- Macro scanning.
- Executable attachment blocking.
- File type filtering.
- Sandboxing of suspicious attachments.
- Automated malware signature updates.
- Behavioral analysis.
- Threat intelligence integration.

Malicious email shall be quarantined or blocked before delivery.

---

# 9. Email Authentication

Enterprise email domains shall implement industry-standard email authentication mechanisms.

Required controls include:

- Sender Policy Framework (SPF).
- DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM).
- Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance (DMARC).
- Domain reputation monitoring.
- Spoofing detection.
- Authentication failure reporting.
- Continuous monitoring of email authentication records.

Email authentication configurations shall be reviewed periodically.

---

# 10. Email Encryption

Sensitive organizational information transmitted through email shall be protected using approved encryption technologies.

Encryption requirements include:

- Transport Layer Security (TLS) for email transmission.
- End-to-end encryption where appropriate.
- Encryption of confidential attachments.
- Secure key management.
- Encryption for regulatory data.
- Digital signatures where required.
- Secure email portals for sensitive communications.

Encryption shall comply with the Enterprise Encryption and Cryptographic Key Management Policy.

---

# 11. Email Access Security

Access to enterprise email systems shall be restricted to authorized users.

Email access security requirements include:

- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Strong password enforcement.
- Single Sign-On (SSO) where supported.
- Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).
- Conditional access policies.
- Session timeout.
- Account lockout after failed login attempts.
- Device compliance verification.
- Secure webmail access.
- Continuous authentication monitoring.

Shared email accounts shall be approved and monitored.

---

# 12. Mobile Email Security

Mobile access to enterprise email shall comply with approved mobile security standards.

Mobile email security requirements include:

- Mobile Device Management (MDM).
- Device encryption.
- Secure screen lock.
- Remote lock capability.
- Remote wipe capability.
- Approved email applications.
- Operating system updates.
- Device compliance monitoring.
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Secure synchronization.

Jailbroken or rooted mobile devices shall not access enterprise email.

---

# 13. Email Retention and Archiving

Enterprise email shall be retained according to business, legal, and regulatory requirements.

Retention requirements include:

- Approved retention schedules.
- Secure email archiving.
- Legal hold support.
- Secure backup.
- Controlled deletion.
- Recovery capabilities.
- Integrity protection.
- Audit logging.
- Periodic retention reviews.
- Secure disposal after retention expiration.

Email records shall comply with applicable legal and regulatory obligations.

---

# 14. Email Monitoring

Enterprise email systems shall be continuously monitored to detect malicious activity and policy violations.

Monitoring activities include:

- Phishing detection.
- Spam detection.
- Malware detection.
- Suspicious login activity.
- Data Loss Prevention (DLP) alerts.
- Email authentication failures.
- Unusual email forwarding rules.
- High-volume outbound email.
- Threat intelligence correlation.
- Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) integration.

Critical security events shall be investigated promptly.

---

# 15. Third-Party Email Services

Third-party email services shall undergo security review before approval.

Security requirements include:

- Vendor risk assessment.
- Security architecture review.
- Contractual security obligations.
- Encryption support.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Logging capabilities.
- Data residency requirements.
- Incident notification requirements.
- Business continuity capabilities.
- Periodic security reassessment.

Third-party email providers shall comply with the Enterprise Third-Party Security Policy.

---

# 16. Email Security Incident Response

Email-related security incidents shall be reported immediately.

Incident response activities include:

- Detection.
- Containment.
- Quarantine of malicious messages.
- Evidence preservation.
- Threat analysis.
- Root cause analysis.
- Recovery.
- Lessons learned.

Email security incidents shall be managed in accordance with the Enterprise Information Security Incident Management Policy.

- Configure secure email gateways.

Employees shall:

- Use organizational email responsibly.
- Protect sensitive information.
- Report suspicious emails immediately.
- Complete required security awareness training.
- Follow approved email usage policies.

# 23. Definitions

**Business Email Compromise (BEC)** – A type of cyberattack in which attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organizations to deceive users into transferring funds or disclosing sensitive information.

**Phishing** – A social engineering attack that uses fraudulent email messages to obtain sensitive information or deliver malicious software.

**SPF (Sender Policy Framework)** – An email authentication protocol used to verify that incoming email originates from an authorized mail server.

**DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)** – An email authentication method that uses cryptographic signatures to verify message integrity and authenticity.

**DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance)** – An email authentication protocol that builds on SPF and DKIM to protect domains from spoofing and phishing.
