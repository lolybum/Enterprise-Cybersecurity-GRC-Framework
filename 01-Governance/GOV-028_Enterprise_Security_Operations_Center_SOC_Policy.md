# GOV-028 Enterprise Security Operations Center (SOC) Policy

**Document ID:** GOV-028

**Version:** 1.0

**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

**Approved By:** Executive Management

**Effective Date:** July 2026

**Review Cycle:** Annual

**Classification:** Internal

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Security Operations Center (SOC) Policy is to establish enterprise-wide requirements for the continuous monitoring, detection, analysis, investigation, response, and reporting of cybersecurity events and incidents.

This policy defines the governance framework for Security Operations Center (SOC) activities to strengthen the organization's cyber defense capabilities, improve threat visibility, reduce response times, and support business resilience.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Security Operations Center (SOC) personnel.
- Information Security teams.
- IT Operations teams.
- Cloud environments.
- On-premises infrastructure.
- Hybrid environments.
- Endpoints.
- Servers.
- Network devices.
- Firewalls.
- Security monitoring platforms.
- Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems.
- Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) platforms.
- Threat intelligence platforms.
- Third-party Managed Security Service Providers (MSSPs).
- All employees, contractors, consultants, vendors, and third parties responsible for monitoring or responding to cybersecurity events.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Continuously monitor enterprise security events.
- Detect cyber threats at the earliest possible stage.
- Improve incident response capabilities.
- Reduce Mean Time to Detect (MTTD).
- Reduce Mean Time to Respond (MTTR).
- Improve cyber threat visibility.
- Support proactive threat hunting.
- Protect enterprise information assets.
- Ensure compliance with regulatory and contractual obligations.

---

# 4. Policy Statement

The organization shall maintain a Security Operations Center (SOC) capability responsible for continuously monitoring enterprise technology environments, detecting malicious activity, coordinating incident response, and improving the organization's cybersecurity posture.

SOC operations shall be supported by approved technologies, documented procedures, trained personnel, and continuous monitoring processes aligned with recognized cybersecurity frameworks and industry best practices.

---

# 5. SOC Operations

The Security Operations Center (SOC) shall operate continuously to detect, analyze, investigate, and coordinate responses to cybersecurity threats.

SOC operational responsibilities include:

- Continuous security monitoring.
- Alert triage.
- Incident investigation.
- Threat intelligence analysis.
- Threat hunting.
- Security event correlation.
- Escalation of critical incidents.
- Coordination with IT Operations.
- Coordination with Incident Response teams.
- Continuous process improvement.

SOC activities shall be documented and regularly reviewed.

---

# 6. Security Monitoring

The SOC shall continuously monitor enterprise technology environments for indicators of compromise and suspicious activity.

Security monitoring shall include:

- Endpoint activity.
- Network traffic.
- Firewall logs.
- Cloud environments.
- Identity and access systems.
- Privileged account activity.
- Application logs.
- Database activity.
- Email security events.
- Third-party service integrations.

Monitoring shall be risk-based and aligned with business priorities.

---

# 7. Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)

The organization shall maintain an approved Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platform.

The SIEM shall support:

- Centralized log collection.
- Event normalization.
- Event correlation.
- Automated alerting.
- Threat intelligence integration.
- Dashboard reporting.
- Log retention.
- Compliance reporting.
- Case management integration.
- Security analytics.

SIEM content shall be reviewed and updated periodically.

---

# 8. Threat Intelligence

Threat intelligence shall be integrated into SOC operations to improve threat detection and response.

Threat intelligence activities include:

- Monitoring threat feeds.
- Monitoring Indicators of Compromise (IOCs).
- Monitoring Indicators of Attack (IOAs).
- Monitoring emerging vulnerabilities.
- Monitoring ransomware campaigns.
- Monitoring phishing trends.
- Monitoring nation-state threats.
- Threat intelligence sharing.
- Threat prioritization.
- Threat reporting.

Threat intelligence sources shall be periodically evaluated for effectiveness.

---

# 9. Threat Hunting

The SOC shall conduct proactive threat hunting activities to identify threats that evade automated detection.

Threat hunting activities include:

- Hypothesis-driven investigations.
- IOC searches.
- Behavioral analytics.
- Endpoint investigations.
- Network traffic analysis.
- Log analysis.
- User behavior analysis.
- Cloud activity analysis.
- Malware hunting.
- Lateral movement detection.

Threat hunting activities shall be documented and reviewed.

---

# 10. Alert Management

Security alerts shall be classified, prioritized, investigated, and resolved according to documented procedures.

Alert management shall include:

- Alert validation.
- Severity classification.
- Risk prioritization.
- False-positive identification.
- Incident creation.
- Escalation procedures.
- Case documentation.
- Root cause analysis.
- Lessons learned.
- Closure verification.

Critical alerts shall receive immediate attention according to the Enterprise Incident Management Policy.

---

# 11. Incident Investigation

The SOC shall investigate security events to determine their scope, impact, and root cause.

Incident investigation activities include:

- Event validation.
- Evidence collection.
- Timeline analysis.
- Root cause analysis.
- Impact assessment.
- Threat actor identification where possible.
- Containment recommendations.
- Recovery coordination.
- Lessons learned.
- Documentation of investigation results.

Investigations shall follow approved Incident Response procedures.

---

# 12. Digital Forensics Support

The SOC shall support digital forensic investigations when required.

Digital forensics activities include:

- Evidence preservation.
- Chain of custody documentation.
- Memory acquisition.
- Disk image collection.
- Log preservation.
- Malware analysis support.
- Endpoint forensic analysis.
- Cloud forensic support.
- Network forensic analysis.
- Legal and regulatory support where applicable.

Digital evidence shall be handled in accordance with approved forensic procedures.

---

# 13. SOC Metrics and Reporting

The SOC shall maintain metrics to measure operational effectiveness and support continuous improvement.

Key performance indicators include:

- Mean Time to Detect (MTTD).
- Mean Time to Respond (MTTR).
- Mean Time to Contain (MTTC).
- Number of security incidents.
- Alert volume.
- False-positive rate.
- Threat hunting activities completed.
- Phishing detection rate.
- Vulnerabilities identified through monitoring.
- SLA compliance.

SOC performance reports shall be provided to executive management on a regular basis.

---

# 14. Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response (SOAR)

The organization shall implement Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response (SOAR) capabilities where appropriate.

SOAR capabilities include:

- Automated alert enrichment.
- Automated case creation.
- Threat intelligence integration.
- Automated playbooks.
- Workflow orchestration.
- Automated notification.
- Automated containment actions.
- Ticketing system integration.
- Reporting automation.
- Analyst workflow optimization.

Automation shall be regularly reviewed to ensure effectiveness and accuracy.

---

# 15. Third-Party SOC Services

Where third-party SOC services or Managed Security Service Providers (MSSPs) are used, they shall meet organizational security requirements.

Third-party SOC requirements include:

- Vendor risk assessment.
- Security due diligence.
- Service Level Agreements (SLAs).
- Defined escalation procedures.
- Continuous monitoring.
- Incident notification requirements.
- Confidentiality obligations.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Periodic performance reviews.
- Contractual security requirements.

Third-party SOC providers shall comply with the Enterprise Third-Party Security Policy.

---

# 16. Continuous Monitoring

The SOC shall maintain continuous monitoring capabilities across enterprise environments.

Continuous monitoring shall include:

- Endpoint monitoring.
- Network monitoring.
- Cloud monitoring.
- Identity monitoring.
- Privileged account monitoring.
- Application monitoring.
- Database monitoring.
- Vulnerability monitoring.
- Threat intelligence monitoring.
- Security posture monitoring.

Monitoring coverage shall be reviewed regularly to address evolving risks and business requirements.

---

# 17. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Management

Executive Management shall:

- Approve the Enterprise Security Operations Center (SOC) Program.
- Allocate appropriate resources.
- Review enterprise cybersecurity risks.
- Support continuous improvement initiatives.

## Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The CISO shall:

- Own this policy.
- Approve SOC standards and procedures.
- Oversee enterprise SOC governance.
- Review SOC performance.
- Report significant cybersecurity risks to Executive Management.

## Security Operations Center (SOC) Team

The SOC Team shall:

- Continuously monitor enterprise environments.
- Investigate security alerts.
- Perform threat hunting.
- Coordinate incident response.
- Maintain SOC documentation.
- Recommend security improvements.
- Produce operational metrics and reports.

## Information Security Team

The Information Security Team shall:

- Develop SOC standards.
- Support threat intelligence activities.
- Conduct security assessments.
- Review monitoring effectiveness.
- Validate security controls.

## IT Operations Team

The IT Operations Team shall:

- Support security monitoring platforms.
- Maintain logging infrastructure.
- Implement corrective actions.
- Assist with incident containment.
- Support system recovery activities.

## Employees

Employees shall:

- Report suspected cybersecurity incidents immediately.
- Cooperate with security investigations.
- Follow approved security policies.
- Complete required security awareness training.

# 18. Compliance

Compliance with this policy shall be verified through:

- Internal audits.
- External audits.
- SOC maturity assessments.
- Incident response reviews.
- Threat hunting reviews.
- SIEM configuration reviews.
- Continuous monitoring.
- Regulatory compliance assessments.

Failure to comply with this policy may result in:

- Corrective action plans.
- Increased monitoring.
- Disciplinary action.
- Contract termination for third parties.
- Legal or regulatory action where applicable.

---

# 19. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy shall:

- Be formally documented.
- Include business justification.
- Include a documented risk assessment.
- Identify compensating security controls.
- Be approved by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO).
- Include an expiration date.
- Be reviewed annually.

Approved exceptions shall be maintained within the Enterprise Exception Register.

# 23. Definitions

**Security Operations Center (SOC)** – A centralized function responsible for continuously monitoring, detecting, analyzing, and responding to cybersecurity threats.

**Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)** – A platform that aggregates, correlates, and analyzes security event data from multiple sources.

**Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response (SOAR)** – A platform that automates security workflows, incident response, and orchestration activities.

**Threat Hunting** – A proactive activity to identify malicious behavior that has evaded automated detection tools.

**Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)** – The average time required to identify a cybersecurity event.

**Mean Time to Respond (MTTR)** – The average time required to contain and remediate a cybersecurity incident.
