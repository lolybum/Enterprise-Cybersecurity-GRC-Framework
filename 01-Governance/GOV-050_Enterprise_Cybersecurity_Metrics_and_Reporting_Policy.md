# GOV-050 Enterprise Cybersecurity Metrics and Reporting Policy

**Document ID:** GOV-050

**Version:** 1.0

**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

**Approved By:** Executive Management

**Effective Date:** July 2026

**Review Cycle:** Annual

**Classification:** Internal

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Cybersecurity Metrics and Reporting Policy is to establish governance requirements for measuring, monitoring, analyzing, and reporting cybersecurity performance, risk, compliance, and operational effectiveness across the organization.

This policy establishes a standardized enterprise framework for developing meaningful cybersecurity metrics that support executive decision-making, improve risk visibility, demonstrate regulatory compliance, measure security program effectiveness, and drive continuous improvement.

---

This policy applies to:

- All employees.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Temporary workers.
- Executive leadership.
- Information Technology personnel.
- Information Security personnel.
- Security Operations Center (SOC) personnel.
- Risk Management personnel.
- Internal Audit.
- Compliance personnel.
- Business owners.
- Third-party service providers supporting cybersecurity operations.

This policy applies to cybersecurity metrics associated with:

- Information Security Governance.
- Risk Management.
- Identity and Access Management (IAM).
- Vulnerability Management.
- Patch Management.
- Security Operations.
- Incident Response.
- Threat Intelligence.

- Cloud Security.

- Disaster recovery.

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Establish enterprise cybersecurity metrics governance.
- Support risk-based decision-making.
- Measure cybersecurity program effectiveness.
- Improve executive visibility into cybersecurity risks.
- Strengthen board-level cybersecurity reporting.
- Support regulatory and audit requirements.
- Monitor security operational performance.
- Measure security control effectiveness.
- Promote accountability across the organization.
- Drive continuous improvement through data-driven decision-making.

---

# 4. Policy Statement

The organization shall maintain a formal Enterprise Cybersecurity Metrics and Reporting Program that defines standardized cybersecurity metrics, reporting processes, governance responsibilities, and performance objectives.

Cybersecurity metrics shall be accurate, timely, risk-based, measurable, repeatable, and aligned with enterprise business objectives and regulatory requirements.

Cybersecurity reporting shall provide meaningful information to operational teams, executive leadership, governance committees, and the Board of Directors to support informed decision-making and effective oversight.

The organization shall periodically review cybersecurity metrics to ensure they remain relevant, actionable, and aligned with the evolving threat landscape, business priorities, and organizational risk appetite.

---

# 5. Cybersecurity Metrics Governance

The organization shall maintain a formal governance framework for defining, collecting, reviewing, and reporting cybersecurity metrics.

Cybersecurity metrics governance shall include:

- Executive sponsorship.
- Defined governance roles and responsibilities.
- Enterprise cybersecurity reporting standards.
- Risk-based metrics management.
- Regulatory compliance oversight.
- Data quality assurance.
- Executive reporting.
- Periodic governance reviews.
- Cross-functional collaboration.
- Continuous improvement initiatives.

Cybersecurity metrics governance shall align with Enterprise Risk Management, Information Security, Internal Audit, Compliance, and Business functions.

---

# 6. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

The organization shall establish Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to measure the effectiveness of cybersecurity operations and security program performance.

Cybersecurity KPIs may include:

- Security awareness training completion rates.
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) adoption rates.
- Patch compliance percentages.
- Vulnerability remediation timelines.
- Endpoint compliance rates.
- Mean Time to Detect (MTTD).
- Mean Time to Respond (MTTR).
- Security assessment completion rates.
- Third-party assessment completion rates.
- Compliance audit success rates.

KPIs shall be reviewed periodically to support operational performance improvement.

---

# 7. Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)

The organization shall establish Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) to measure cybersecurity risk exposure.

Cybersecurity KRIs may include:

- Number of critical vulnerabilities.
- Number of overdue security patches.
- Privileged account exceptions.
- High-risk third-party vendors.
- Phishing susceptibility rates.
- Unauthorized access attempts.
- Security incident frequency.
- Cloud misconfiguration findings.
- Regulatory compliance gaps.
- High-risk audit findings.

KRIs shall support enterprise risk reporting and executive decision-making.

---

# 8. Executive Dashboards

Cybersecurity metrics shall be presented through executive dashboards to provide leadership with visibility into cybersecurity posture.

Executive dashboards may include:

- Enterprise cybersecurity risk score.
- Security program maturity.
- Open critical vulnerabilities.
- Incident trends.
- Threat intelligence trends.
- Third-party risk ratings.
- Compliance status.
- Security awareness metrics.
- Cloud security posture.
- Operational security metrics.

Dashboards shall be updated on a defined reporting schedule.

---

# 9. Board Reporting

The Board of Directors or designated governance committee shall receive periodic cybersecurity reports.

Board reporting shall include:

- Enterprise cyber risk overview.
- Significant cybersecurity incidents.
- Regulatory compliance status.
- Security investment priorities.
- Third-party risk exposure.
- Zero Trust maturity.
- Cybersecurity program maturity.
- AI security governance updates.
- Business continuity readiness.
- Emerging cyber threats.

Reports shall support strategic oversight and governance responsibilities.

---

# 10. Compliance Metrics

Compliance metrics shall be maintained to demonstrate adherence to applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and internal policy requirements.

Compliance metrics may include:

- Policy compliance rates.
- Regulatory audit results.
- Internal audit findings.
- External audit findings.
- Risk assessment completion rates.
- Control testing results.
- Privacy compliance metrics.
- Vendor compliance status.
- Exception management metrics.
- Corrective action completion rates.

Compliance metrics shall be reviewed periodically and reported to appropriate governance bodies.

---

# 11. Operational Security Metrics

The organization shall maintain operational security metrics to measure the effectiveness and efficiency of cybersecurity operations.

Operational security metrics may include:

- Security event volume.
- Security alert volume.
- Security alert triage time.
- Mean Time to Detect (MTTD).
- Mean Time to Respond (MTTR).
- Mean Time to Contain (MTTC).
- Security incident closure rates.
- Security Operations Center (SOC) performance.
- Threat hunting activities.
- Security automation effectiveness.

Operational metrics shall support continuous improvement of cybersecurity operations.

---

# 12. Vulnerability Metrics

The organization shall monitor vulnerability management metrics to evaluate exposure to known security weaknesses.

Vulnerability metrics may include:

- Total vulnerabilities identified.
- Critical vulnerability count.
- High-risk vulnerability count.
- Vulnerability remediation timelines.
- Vulnerability aging.
- Patch compliance rates.
- Recurring vulnerabilities.
- Internet-facing vulnerabilities.
- Vulnerability assessment coverage.
- Vulnerability scan frequency.

Vulnerability metrics shall support enterprise risk reduction initiatives.

---

# 13. Incident Metrics

Cybersecurity incident metrics shall be maintained to evaluate incident management performance.

Incident metrics may include:

- Number of security incidents.
- Incident severity distribution.
- Mean Time to Detect (MTTD).
- Mean Time to Respond (MTTR).
- Mean Time to Recover (MTTR).
- Incident containment effectiveness.
- Root cause trends.
- Repeat incident frequency.
- Regulatory notification metrics.
- Lessons learned implementation status.

Incident metrics shall support continual improvement of incident response capabilities.

---

# 14. Third-Party Risk Metrics

The organization shall maintain metrics to monitor cybersecurity risks associated with third-party vendors.

Third-party metrics may include:

- Vendor risk ratings.
- Critical vendor inventory.
- Vendor assessment completion rates.
- Open vendor remediation items.
- High-risk vendor exceptions.
- Vendor security incidents.
- Vendor compliance status.
- Fourth-party risk exposure.
- Contract review completion rates.
- Vendor offboarding completion rates.

Third-party metrics shall support enterprise Vendor Risk Management (VRM) governance.

---

# 15. Continuous Monitoring

Cybersecurity metrics shall be continuously monitored to identify trends, emerging risks, and opportunities for improvement.

Continuous monitoring activities include:

- Automated metric collection.
- Security event monitoring.
- Dashboard updates.
- Risk trend analysis.
- Compliance monitoring.
- Threat intelligence integration.
- Executive reporting.
- Data quality validation.
- Metric review meetings.
- Continuous improvement initiatives.

Monitoring frequency shall be based on business criticality and organizational risk tolerance.

---

# 16. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Management

Executive Management shall:

- Approve the Enterprise Cybersecurity Metrics and Reporting Program.
- Review enterprise cybersecurity performance.
- Allocate appropriate resources.
- Support continuous improvement initiatives.

## Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The CISO shall:

- Own this policy.
- Approve cybersecurity metrics and reporting standards.
- Review cybersecurity performance metrics.
- Present cybersecurity reports to Executive Management and the Board.
- Report significant cybersecurity risks.

## Information Security Team

The Information Security Team shall:

- Collect cybersecurity metrics.
- Validate data quality.
- Produce cybersecurity dashboards.
- Analyze cybersecurity trends.
- Recommend performance improvements.
- Maintain reporting documentation.

## Security Operations Center (SOC)

The Security Operations Center shall:

- Monitor operational security metrics.
- Report incident metrics.
- Support threat intelligence reporting.
- Maintain security event data.
- Escalate significant operational issues.

## Internal Audit and Compliance

Internal Audit and Compliance shall:

- Validate cybersecurity reporting processes.
- Review compliance metrics.
- Assess reporting effectiveness.
- Support regulatory reporting.
- Recommend governance improvements.

## Business Owners

Business Owners shall:

- Review cybersecurity reports relevant to their operations.
- Support remediation activities.
- Provide accurate business information.
- Participate in governance reviews.
- Report emerging cybersecurity risks.

# 18. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy shall:

- Be formally documented.
- Include business justification.
- Include a documented risk assessment.
- Identify compensating controls.
- Be approved by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO).
- Include an expiration date.
- Be reviewed annually.

Approved exceptions shall be maintained within the Enterprise Exception Register.

# 22. Definitions

**Cybersecurity Metric** – A measurable value used to evaluate the effectiveness, efficiency, or performance of cybersecurity controls, processes, or programs.

**Key Performance Indicator (KPI)** – A measurable indicator used to evaluate the achievement of cybersecurity operational or strategic objectives.

**Key Risk Indicator (KRI)** – A measurable indicator used to identify changes in cybersecurity risk exposure or emerging threats.

**Executive Dashboard** – A consolidated visual presentation of cybersecurity metrics designed to support executive decision-making and governance oversight.

**Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)** – The average time required to identify a cybersecurity incident after it occurs.

**Mean Time to Respond (MTTR)** – The average time required to respond to and contain a cybersecurity incident after detection.
