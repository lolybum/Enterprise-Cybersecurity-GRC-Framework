# GOV-038 Enterprise Disaster Recovery Policy

**Document ID:** GOV-038

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Disaster Recovery Policy is to establish governance requirements for the recovery of enterprise information systems, infrastructure, applications, networks, and supporting technology following disasters or disruptive events that significantly affect business operations.

This policy establishes a standardized Disaster Recovery (DR) framework that enables the organization to restore critical technology services in a timely manner, minimize operational disruption, and maintain the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of organizational information.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Temporary workers.
- Third-party vendors.
- Managed service providers.
- Cloud environments.
- On-premises environments.
- Hybrid environments.
- Data centers.
- Servers.
- Networks.
- Storage systems.
- Databases.
- Applications.
- Virtual machines.
- Containers.
- Backup repositories.
- Disaster recovery sites.

This policy applies to all enterprise technology assets that support critical business operations.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Ensure timely recovery of enterprise technology services.
- Minimize operational disruption.
- Protect critical business systems and data.
- Establish standardized disaster recovery procedures.
- Support Business Continuity Management.
- Improve cyber resilience.
- Reduce financial and operational impacts.
- Support regulatory and contractual compliance.
- Integrate disaster recovery with enterprise risk management.

---

# 4. Policy Statement

The organization shall maintain a formal Enterprise Disaster Recovery (DR) Program to ensure the timely restoration of critical technology services following disasters, cyber incidents, infrastructure failures, or other disruptive events.

Disaster recovery planning shall be risk-based, documented, tested regularly, and integrated with Business Continuity, Backup and Recovery, Incident Management, Cybersecurity, and Enterprise Risk Management programs.

Disaster Recovery Plans shall be reviewed, updated, and approved at least annually or following significant organizational, operational, or technology changes.

---

# 5. Disaster Recovery Governance

The organization shall maintain a formal Disaster Recovery (DR) governance structure to oversee disaster recovery planning, implementation, testing, maintenance, and continuous improvement.

Disaster recovery governance shall include:

- Executive sponsorship.
- Disaster Recovery Steering Committee.
- Defined governance roles and responsibilities.
- Disaster recovery policies and standards.
- Enterprise-wide coordination.
- Risk-based decision-making.
- Performance monitoring.
- Regulatory compliance oversight.
- Periodic management reviews.
- Continuous improvement initiatives.

Disaster recovery governance shall align with Enterprise Risk Management, Business Continuity Management, and Information Security governance.

---

# 6. Disaster Recovery Planning

The organization shall maintain documented Disaster Recovery Plans (DRPs) for all critical information systems and supporting technology.

Disaster Recovery Plans shall include:

- Recovery scope.
- Recovery priorities.
- Technology dependencies.
- Infrastructure recovery procedures.
- Application recovery procedures.
- Database recovery procedures.
- Network recovery procedures.
- Recovery team responsibilities.
- Communication procedures.
- Recovery validation activities.

Disaster Recovery Plans shall be reviewed and approved annually.

---

# 7. Recovery Sites

Recovery capabilities shall include appropriate alternate processing locations based on business requirements and risk assessments.

Recovery site strategies may include:

- Hot sites.
- Warm sites.
- Cold sites.
- Cloud recovery environments.
- Secondary data centers.
- Geographic redundancy.
- Hybrid recovery solutions.
- Co-location facilities.
- Virtual recovery environments.
- Mobile recovery capabilities.

Recovery sites shall be protected using security controls equivalent to those applied at primary production sites.

---

# 8. Recovery Time Objectives (RTO)

Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs) shall be established for all critical systems and technology services.

RTO planning shall include:

- Business priority classification.
- Maximum acceptable downtime.
- Infrastructure recovery sequencing.
- Application recovery sequencing.
- Resource availability.
- Staffing requirements.
- Vendor dependencies.
- Executive approval.
- Documentation updates.
- Periodic validation.

Approved RTOs shall align with Business Continuity Plans.

---

# 9. Recovery Point Objectives (RPO)

Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs) shall define the maximum acceptable amount of data loss for enterprise systems.

RPO planning shall include:

- Data criticality assessment.
- Backup frequency.
- Replication frequency.
- Database recovery capabilities.
- Storage redundancy.
- Cloud recovery capabilities.
- Regulatory obligations.
- Business owner approval.
- Documentation updates.
- Periodic validation.

RPOs shall align with enterprise backup and recovery capabilities.

---

# 10. Disaster Declaration Procedures

The organization shall establish formal procedures for declaring a disaster and activating Disaster Recovery Plans.

Disaster declaration procedures shall include:

- Incident assessment.
- Executive notification.
- Disaster declaration authority.
- Activation criteria.
- Recovery team mobilization.
- Stakeholder notification.
- Regulatory notifications where required.
- Vendor coordination.
- Recovery prioritization.
- Documentation of declaration activities.

Only authorized personnel may declare a disaster and activate enterprise Disaster Recovery Plans.

---

# 11. Recovery Procedures

Documented recovery procedures shall be maintained for all critical enterprise technology services.

Recovery procedures shall include:

- Infrastructure recovery.
- Network recovery.
- Server recovery.
- Database recovery.
- Application recovery.
- Identity and Access Management (IAM) recovery.
- Security control restoration.
- Backup restoration.
- System validation.
- Recovery documentation.

Recovery procedures shall be reviewed and updated following major technology changes or recovery exercises.

---

# 12. Disaster Recovery Testing

The organization shall conduct periodic Disaster Recovery (DR) testing to validate recovery capabilities and identify improvement opportunities.

Testing activities include:

- Tabletop exercises.
- Functional recovery testing.
- Full-scale disaster recovery exercises.
- Backup restoration testing.
- Application recovery testing.
- Database recovery testing.
- Network recovery testing.
- Cloud recovery testing.
- Documentation of test results.
- Corrective action tracking.

Critical Disaster Recovery Plans shall be tested at least annually.

---

# 13. Cloud Disaster Recovery

Cloud-hosted systems shall implement disaster recovery capabilities appropriate to the criticality of the services provided.

Cloud disaster recovery requirements include:

- Multi-region deployment where appropriate.
- Cloud backup integration.
- Cross-region replication.
- High availability architecture.
- Disaster recovery automation.
- Recovery validation.
- Access control protection.
- Encryption of replicated data.
- Periodic recovery testing.
- Compliance with contractual obligations.

Cloud disaster recovery shall align with the Enterprise Cloud Security Governance Policy.

---

# 14. Third-Party Disaster Recovery

Critical third-party service providers shall maintain disaster recovery capabilities that support organizational resilience.

Third-party requirements include:

- Documented Disaster Recovery Plans.
- Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs).
- Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs).
- Periodic recovery testing.
- Service Level Agreements (SLAs).
- Vendor risk assessments.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Incident notification procedures.
- Audit rights.
- Continuous performance monitoring.

Third-party disaster recovery capabilities shall align with the Enterprise Third-Party Security Policy.

---

# 15. Monitoring and Reporting

Disaster recovery readiness shall be continuously monitored and reported.

Monitoring activities include:

- Disaster recovery testing results.
- Recovery objective performance.
- Backup success rates.
- Recovery exercise findings.
- Disaster recovery plan reviews.
- Infrastructure resilience metrics.
- Executive reporting.
- Audit logging.
- Regulatory reporting where required.
- Continuous improvement tracking.

Significant disaster recovery deficiencies shall be documented, prioritized, and remediated.

---

# 16. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Management

Executive Management shall:

- Approve the Enterprise Disaster Recovery Program.
- Allocate appropriate resources.
- Review enterprise resilience risks.
- Support continuous improvement initiatives.

## Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The CISO shall:

- Own this policy.
- Oversee enterprise disaster recovery governance.
- Review disaster recovery metrics.
- Coordinate with Business Continuity and Incident Management teams.
- Report significant resilience risks to Executive Management.

## Disaster Recovery Manager

The Disaster Recovery Manager shall:

- Develop and maintain Disaster Recovery Plans.
- Coordinate disaster recovery testing.
- Maintain recovery documentation.
- Track corrective actions.
- Coordinate enterprise recovery activities.
- Report recovery readiness.

## Information Technology Team

The Information Technology Team shall:

- Maintain recovery infrastructure.
- Perform backup and restoration activities.
- Execute recovery procedures.
- Validate recovered systems.
- Support disaster recovery testing.
- Maintain technical recovery documentation.

## Information Security Team

The Information Security Team shall:

- Assess disaster recovery security controls.
- Monitor cyber resilience.
- Participate in recovery exercises.
- Review recovery security risks.
- Recommend security improvements.

## System Owners

System Owners shall:

- Identify critical systems.
- Define Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs).
- Participate in disaster recovery testing.
- Validate restored systems.
- Ensure Disaster Recovery Plans remain current.

Exceptions to this policy shall:

- Be formally documented.
- Include business justification.
- Include a documented risk assessment.

# 22. Definitions

**Disaster Recovery (DR)** – The processes, procedures, and technologies used to restore information systems, infrastructure, and applications following a disruptive event.

**Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)** – A documented plan describing the procedures required to recover technology services after a disaster.

**Recovery Time Objective (RTO)** – The maximum acceptable amount of time required to restore a system or service following an interruption.

**Recovery Point Objective (RPO)** – The maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time before an interruption.

**Recovery Site** – An alternate processing location used to restore technology services following a disaster.

**Disaster Declaration** – The formal decision by authorized leadership to activate Disaster Recovery Plans following a qualifying disruptive event.
