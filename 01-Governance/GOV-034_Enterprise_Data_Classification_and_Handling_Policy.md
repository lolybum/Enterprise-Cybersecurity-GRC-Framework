# GOV-034 Enterprise Data Classification and Handling Policy

**Document ID:** GOV-034

**Version:** 1.0

**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

**Approved By:** Executive Management

**Effective Date:** July 2026

**Review Cycle:** Annual

**Classification:** Internal

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Data Classification and Handling Policy is to establish governance requirements for classifying, labeling, handling, storing, transmitting, retaining, and disposing of organizational information based on its sensitivity, business value, and regulatory obligations.

This policy provides a consistent enterprise-wide framework for protecting information assets throughout their lifecycle while supporting confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy, and compliance with applicable legal, contractual, and regulatory requirements.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Temporary workers.
- Third-party vendors.
- Managed service providers.
- Business partners.
- Cloud environments.
- On-premises environments.
- Hybrid environments.
- Structured and unstructured data.
- Electronic records.
- Paper records.
- Databases.
- Applications.
- Email.
- Mobile devices.
- Backup media.
- Removable media.

This policy applies to all organizational information regardless of format, storage location, or transmission method.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Establish a standardized enterprise data classification framework.
- Protect sensitive organizational information.
- Reduce the risk of unauthorized disclosure.
- Support regulatory and contractual compliance.
- Improve information governance.
- Enable secure information sharing.
- Define data handling responsibilities.
- Strengthen data protection throughout the information lifecycle.
- Promote consistent data security practices across the enterprise.

---

# 4. Policy Statement

The organization shall classify all information assets according to their sensitivity, business value, legal obligations, and operational importance.

Appropriate administrative, technical, and physical safeguards shall be applied throughout the information lifecycle to ensure that organizational information is protected from unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, destruction, or loss.

Information shall only be accessed, used, transmitted, shared, retained, and disposed of in accordance with its assigned classification and approved enterprise security standards.

---

# 5. Data Classification Levels

The organization shall classify information using the following enterprise data classification levels:

## Public

Information approved for public disclosure.

Examples include:

- Public website content.
- Marketing materials.
- Press releases.
- Published financial reports.
- Public job postings.

Public information requires minimal protection but shall remain accurate and authorized for release.

---

## Internal

Information intended for internal organizational use.

Examples include:

- Internal procedures.
- Employee directories.
- Internal communications.
- Standard operating procedures.
- Internal project documentation.

Internal information shall not be disclosed externally without authorization.

---

## Confidential

Information that could negatively affect the organization if disclosed without authorization.

Examples include:

- Customer information.
- Financial records.
- Contracts.
- Vendor agreements.
- Human Resources records.
- Security documentation.
- Internal audit reports.

Confidential information shall only be accessible to authorized personnel with a legitimate business need.

---

## Restricted

The organization's most sensitive information requiring the highest level of protection.

Examples include:

- Authentication credentials.
- Cryptographic keys.
- Regulated personal information.
- Payment card data.
- Protected health information.
- Trade secrets.
- Merger and acquisition information.
- Security incident investigation evidence.

Restricted information shall be protected using enhanced administrative, technical, and physical security controls.

---

# 6. Data Ownership

Each information asset shall have an assigned Data Owner responsible for its classification and protection.

Data Owner responsibilities include:

- Determining classification levels.
- Approving access.
- Reviewing classification periodically.
- Defining handling requirements.
- Supporting compliance.
- Approving information sharing.
- Reviewing retention requirements.
- Supporting risk assessments.
- Authorizing exceptions.
- Coordinating with Information Security.

Data ownership responsibilities may be delegated but accountability remains with the designated Data Owner.

---

# 7. Data Labeling

Information shall be labeled according to its classification whenever technically and operationally feasible.

Labeling requirements include:

- Electronic document labeling.
- Email classification labels.
- Database classification.
- File classification.
- Physical document markings.
- Storage media labels.
- Backup media labeling.
- Cloud storage labels.
- Automated classification where supported.
- Periodic label validation.

Labeling shall remain consistent throughout the information lifecycle.

---

# 8. Data Handling Requirements

Information shall be handled according to its assigned classification.

Handling requirements include:

- Access restrictions.
- Need-to-know principles.
- Least privilege access.
- Secure processing.
- Secure printing.
- Secure copying.
- Secure storage.
- Secure destruction.
- Protection against unauthorized disclosure.
- Documentation of handling procedures.

Employees shall receive training on approved information handling procedures.

---

# 9. Data Storage Requirements

Organizational information shall be stored using approved enterprise storage solutions.

Storage requirements include:

- Approved storage locations.
- Encryption of Confidential and Restricted data where required.
- Access controls.
- Backup protection.
- Secure cloud storage.
- Physical security controls.
- Media protection.
- Redundancy where appropriate.
- Disaster recovery support.
- Continuous monitoring.

Unauthorized storage of enterprise information on personal devices or unapproved cloud services is prohibited unless specifically authorized.

---

# 10. Data Transmission Requirements

Information transmitted electronically or physically shall be protected according to its classification.

Transmission requirements include:

- Encryption for Confidential and Restricted information.
- Secure email transmission.
- Secure file transfer.
- VPN protection for remote access where applicable.
- Approved collaboration platforms.
- Secure API communications.
- Verification of recipients.
- Protection against interception.
- Logging of sensitive transfers where appropriate.
- Compliance with applicable regulatory requirements.

Sensitive information shall not be transmitted through unapproved communication channels.

---

# 11. Data Sharing

Organizational information shall only be shared with authorized individuals and entities for legitimate business purposes.

Data sharing requirements include:

- Business justification.
- Data Owner approval where required.
- Need-to-know principles.
- Least privilege access.
- Secure collaboration platforms.
- Confidentiality agreements where applicable.
- Regulatory compliance verification.
- Recipient identity verification.
- Secure transmission methods.
- Documentation of high-risk data sharing activities.

Sharing of Confidential or Restricted information shall be limited to authorized recipients.

---

# 12. Third-Party Data Handling

Third parties that access, process, store, or transmit organizational information shall comply with enterprise information protection requirements.

Third-party requirements include:

- Contractual security obligations.
- Data processing agreements.
- Vendor risk assessments.
- Security due diligence.
- Encryption requirements.
- Access control requirements.
- Data retention obligations.
- Secure disposal requirements.
- Incident notification obligations.
- Periodic compliance reviews.

Third-party data handling shall align with the Enterprise Third-Party Security Policy.

---

# 13. Data Retention

Information shall be retained according to business, legal, contractual, and regulatory requirements.

Retention requirements include:

- Approved retention schedules.
- Legal hold procedures.
- Regulatory retention requirements.
- Business record retention.
- Secure archival.
- Backup retention.
- Periodic retention reviews.
- Documentation of retention decisions.
- Controlled deletion.
- Compliance monitoring.

Information shall not be retained longer than necessary unless required by law or approved business requirements.

---

# 14. Secure Disposal

Information shall be securely destroyed when retention requirements have been satisfied.

Secure disposal methods include:

- Secure electronic deletion.
- Cryptographic erasure where applicable.
- Media sanitization.
- Secure shredding of paper records.
- Destruction of removable media.
- Destruction verification.
- Certificate of destruction where appropriate.
- Chain of custody documentation.
- Secure disposal by approved vendors.
- Compliance with applicable regulatory requirements.

Disposal activities shall be documented for Confidential and Restricted information.

---

# 15. Monitoring and Auditing

Information handling activities shall be monitored and audited to ensure compliance with this policy.

Monitoring activities include:

- Data access monitoring.
- Data Loss Prevention (DLP) monitoring.
- Unauthorized access detection.
- File integrity monitoring.
- Data classification compliance reviews.
- Audit logging.
- Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) integration.
- User activity monitoring where appropriate.
- Periodic internal audits.
- Regulatory compliance assessments.

Monitoring results shall be reviewed regularly by Information Security.

---

# 16. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Management

Executive Management shall:

- Approve the Enterprise Data Classification Program.
- Allocate appropriate resources.
- Review enterprise information protection risks.
- Support continuous improvement initiatives.

## Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The CISO shall:

- Own this policy.
- Oversee enterprise data governance.
- Approve classification exceptions.
- Review enterprise data protection metrics.
- Report significant information protection risks to Executive Management.

## Data Owners

Data Owners shall:

- Classify information assets.
- Approve access requests.
- Define handling requirements.
- Review classifications periodically.
- Ensure appropriate retention.
- Authorize information sharing where required.

## Information Security Team

The Information Security Team shall:

- Develop enterprise data protection standards.
- Monitor compliance.
- Conduct information protection assessments.
- Investigate information security incidents.
- Recommend security improvements.
- Support regulatory compliance activities.

## Information Technology Team

The Information Technology Team shall:

- Implement technical security controls.
- Maintain secure storage solutions.
- Support encryption technologies.
- Protect backup media.
- Implement monitoring capabilities.
- Support secure disposal activities.

## Employees

Employees shall:

- Handle information according to its classification.
- Protect organizational information.
- Report suspected information security incidents.
- Complete required security awareness training.
- Follow approved information handling procedures.

# 17. Compliance

Compliance with this policy shall be verified through:

- Internal audits.
- External audits.
- Data classification assessments.
- Data handling compliance reviews.
- Data Loss Prevention (DLP) assessments.
- Information Security assessments.
- Continuous monitoring.
- Regulatory compliance reviews.
- Third-party security assessments.
- Executive management reporting.

Failure to comply with this policy may result in:

- Corrective action plans.
- Removal of unauthorized access.
- Increased monitoring.
- Disciplinary action.
- Contract termination for third parties.
- Legal or regulatory action where applicable.

---

# 18. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy shall:

- Be formally documented.
- Include business justification.
- Include a documented risk assessment.
- Identify compensating security controls.
- Be approved by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO).
- Include an expiration date.
- Be reviewed annually.

Approved exceptions shall be maintained within the Enterprise Exception Register.

---

The Enterprise Data Classification and Handling Program shall be continuously improved through:

- Internal audits.
- External audits.
- Data governance assessments.
- Lessons learned from security incidents.
- Regulatory updates.
- Threat intelligence.
- Data protection trend analysis.
- Technology modernization.
- Industry best practices.
- Executive management reviews.

Program effectiveness shall be reviewed annually.

# 22. Definitions

**Data Classification** – The process of categorizing information according to its sensitivity, business value, and regulatory requirements.

**Data Owner** – The individual responsible for determining the classification, protection requirements, and appropriate use of organizational information.

**Confidential Information** – Information that could negatively impact the organization if disclosed without authorization.

**Restricted Information** – The organization's most sensitive information requiring the highest level of protection.

**Data Loss Prevention (DLP)** – Technologies and processes designed to detect and prevent the unauthorized disclosure, transmission, or loss of sensitive information.

**Media Sanitization** – The process of securely removing or destroying data from storage media to prevent unauthorized recovery.
