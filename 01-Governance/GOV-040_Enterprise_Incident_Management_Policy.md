# GOV-040 Enterprise Incident Management Policy

**Document ID:** GOV-040

**Version:** 1.0

**Owner:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

**Approved By:** Executive Management

**Effective Date:** July 2026

**Review Cycle:** Annual

**Classification:** Internal

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Incident Management Policy is to establish governance requirements for identifying, reporting, assessing, responding to, containing, eradicating, recovering from, and documenting cybersecurity incidents that may affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of organizational information systems and data.

This policy establishes a standardized Enterprise Incident Management framework to ensure cybersecurity incidents are managed consistently, efficiently, and effectively while minimizing business disruption, protecting organizational assets, supporting regulatory compliance, and strengthening organizational resilience.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Temporary workers.
- Third-party vendors.
- Managed service providers.
- Cloud environments.
- On-premises environments.
- Hybrid environments.
- Servers.
- Endpoints.
- Applications.
- Databases.
- Networks.
- Identity and Access Management (IAM) platforms.
- Security monitoring platforms.
- Business systems.
- Information assets.

This policy applies to all suspected or confirmed cybersecurity incidents involving enterprise technology or information assets.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Establish standardized incident management processes.
- Improve incident detection and reporting.
- Minimize the impact of cybersecurity incidents.
- Support rapid containment and recovery.
- Preserve evidence for forensic investigations.
- Meet legal, regulatory, and contractual obligations.
- Improve organizational cyber resilience.
- Support continuous improvement through lessons learned.
- Integrate incident management with enterprise risk management.

---

# 4. Policy Statement

The organization shall maintain a formal Enterprise Incident Management Program to identify, assess, respond to, contain, eradicate, recover from, and document cybersecurity incidents.

Incident management activities shall be risk-based, documented, regularly tested, and integrated with the Security Operations Center (SOC), Business Continuity, Disaster Recovery, Vulnerability Management, Threat Intelligence, and Enterprise Risk Management programs.

All employees shall promptly report suspected cybersecurity incidents using approved reporting channels. Incident response activities shall follow documented procedures designed to minimize operational impact while preserving evidence and supporting timely recovery.

---

# 5. Incident Management Governance

The organization shall maintain a formal Incident Management governance structure to oversee the identification, reporting, response, recovery, and continuous improvement of cybersecurity incident management activities.

Incident management governance shall include:

- Executive sponsorship.
- Defined governance roles and responsibilities.
- Incident response policies and standards.
- Risk-based decision-making.
- Enterprise-wide coordination.
- Regulatory compliance oversight.
- Executive reporting.
- Performance monitoring.
- Periodic management reviews.
- Continuous improvement initiatives.

Incident management governance shall align with Enterprise Risk Management, Business Continuity, Disaster Recovery, and Security Operations Center (SOC) governance.

---

# 6. Incident Classification

Cybersecurity incidents shall be classified according to their impact, severity, urgency, and business risk.

Incident categories include:

- Malware and ransomware.
- Phishing and social engineering.
- Unauthorized access.
- Privilege misuse.
- Insider threats.
- Denial-of-Service (DoS) and Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS).
- Data breaches.
- System compromise.
- Cloud security incidents.
- Third-party security incidents.

Incident classification shall be reviewed throughout the lifecycle of the incident as additional information becomes available.

---

# 7. Incident Reporting

All suspected or confirmed cybersecurity incidents shall be reported immediately using approved reporting channels.

Reporting requirements include:

- Immediate reporting of suspected incidents.
- Reporting through approved service desk or incident management systems.
- Notification to the Security Operations Center (SOC).
- Escalation of critical incidents.
- Reporting of third-party security incidents.
- Reporting of data privacy incidents.
- Documentation of incident details.
- Preservation of available evidence.
- Timely executive notification where appropriate.
- Regulatory notification when required.

Failure to report known incidents may result in disciplinary action.

---

# 8. Incident Assessment

Reported incidents shall be assessed to determine their scope, impact, severity, and required response actions.

Assessment activities include:

- Validation of the reported event.
- Impact analysis.
- Affected asset identification.
- Business impact evaluation.
- Threat intelligence correlation.
- Vulnerability assessment.
- Root cause analysis.
- Regulatory impact review.
- Assignment of incident severity.
- Determination of escalation requirements.

Assessment findings shall be documented within the enterprise incident management system.

---

# 9. Incident Response Process

The organization shall maintain a documented incident response process based on recognized industry frameworks.

The incident response lifecycle shall include:

- Preparation.
- Detection and analysis.
- Containment.
- Eradication.
- Recovery.
- Post-incident review.
- Lessons learned.
- Documentation.
- Corrective actions.
- Continuous improvement.

Incident response activities shall follow documented procedures and approved playbooks where available.

---

# 10. Incident Severity Levels

Cybersecurity incidents shall be assigned severity levels to support consistent prioritization and response.

Severity levels include:

### Critical

Incidents causing severe business disruption, widespread compromise, significant regulatory impact, or major data loss requiring immediate executive attention.

### High

Incidents affecting critical business systems or sensitive information that require rapid response.

### Medium

Incidents with limited operational impact that require timely investigation and remediation.

### Low

Minor security events with minimal business impact that can be addressed through standard operational procedures.

Severity classifications shall be reviewed throughout the incident lifecycle as additional information becomes available.

---

# 11. Incident Containment

The organization shall implement containment measures to limit the impact of cybersecurity incidents and prevent further compromise.

Containment activities include:

- Isolation of affected systems.
- Network segmentation.
- Blocking malicious IP addresses.
- Disabling compromised accounts.
- Revoking unauthorized access.
- Quarantining infected endpoints.
- Temporary service restrictions.
- Preservation of forensic evidence.
- Communication with affected stakeholders.
- Documentation of containment actions.

Containment actions shall be appropriate to the severity and nature of the incident while minimizing disruption to business operations.

---

# 12. Incident Eradication

Following successful containment, the organization shall remove the root cause of the cybersecurity incident.

Eradication activities include:

- Malware removal.
- Elimination of unauthorized accounts.
- Vulnerability remediation.
- Patch deployment.
- Credential resets.
- Configuration corrections.
- Removal of malicious software.
- Validation of system integrity.
- Confirmation of threat elimination.
- Documentation of eradication activities.

All eradication activities shall be validated before recovery begins.

---

# 13. Recovery Procedures

The organization shall restore affected systems and services in a controlled manner following eradication activities.

Recovery activities include:

- System restoration.
- Data restoration from approved backups.
- Validation of restored services.
- Security testing.
- User acceptance validation where appropriate.
- Monitoring for recurring malicious activity.
- Business owner approval.
- Controlled return to production.
- Documentation of recovery activities.
- Post-recovery monitoring.

Recovered systems shall be closely monitored for signs of recurring compromise.

---

# 14. Digital Forensics and Evidence Handling

The organization shall preserve digital evidence to support investigations, legal proceedings, and regulatory obligations.

Evidence handling requirements include:

- Evidence identification.
- Evidence preservation.
- Chain of custody documentation.
- Secure evidence storage.
- Forensic imaging where appropriate.
- Integrity verification.
- Restricted evidence access.
- Documentation of evidence handling.
- Legal coordination where required.
- Compliance with applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

Digital evidence shall be handled in a manner that preserves its integrity and admissibility.

---

# 15. Communication and Notifications

The organization shall maintain documented communication procedures for cybersecurity incidents.

Communication requirements include:

- Internal stakeholder notifications.
- Executive notifications.
- Security Operations Center (SOC) notifications.
- Legal notifications.
- Privacy notifications where applicable.
- Customer notifications when required.
- Regulatory notifications.
- Law enforcement coordination where appropriate.
- Third-party notifications.
- Public communications through authorized spokespersons only.

Communications shall be timely, accurate, and coordinated to ensure consistency and compliance.

---

# 16. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Management

Executive Management shall:

- Approve the Enterprise Incident Management Program.
- Allocate appropriate resources.
- Review significant cybersecurity incidents.
- Support continuous improvement initiatives.

## Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The CISO shall:

- Own this policy.
- Oversee enterprise incident management governance.
- Approve incident response strategies.
- Review incident metrics.
- Report significant cybersecurity risks to Executive Management.

## Security Operations Center (SOC)

The Security Operations Center (SOC) shall:

- Monitor enterprise security events.
- Detect and analyze cybersecurity incidents.
- Coordinate incident response activities.
- Escalate incidents based on severity.
- Maintain incident documentation.
- Conduct post-incident reviews.

## Incident Response Team

The Incident Response Team shall:

- Investigate cybersecurity incidents.
- Perform containment and eradication activities.
- Coordinate recovery efforts.
- Preserve forensic evidence.
- Document incident response actions.
- Recommend corrective actions.

## Information Technology Team

The Information Technology Team shall:

- Support containment and recovery activities.
- Restore affected systems.
- Apply security patches and configuration changes.
- Validate system functionality after recovery.
- Assist with technical investigations.

## Employees

Employees shall:

- Report suspected cybersecurity incidents immediately.
- Cooperate with incident response activities.
- Preserve potential evidence.
- Follow incident response procedures.
- Complete required incident response awareness training.

# 18. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy shall:

- Be formally documented.
- Include business justification.
- Include a documented risk assessment.
- Identify compensating security controls.
- Be approved by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO).
- Include an expiration date.
- Be reviewed annually.

Approved exceptions shall be maintained within the Enterprise Exception Register.

# 22. Definitions

**Cybersecurity Incident** – An event that actually or potentially jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information systems, information assets, or business operations.

**Incident Response** – The structured process used to prepare for, detect, analyze, contain, eradicate, recover from, and learn from cybersecurity incidents.

**Security Operations Center (SOC)** – The organizational function responsible for continuously monitoring, detecting, investigating, and responding to cybersecurity events and incidents.

**Digital Forensics** – The collection, preservation, analysis, and presentation of digital evidence to support investigations and legal or regulatory requirements.

**Chain of Custody** – Documentation that records the collection, handling, transfer, storage, and disposition of evidence to preserve its integrity.

**Lessons Learned Review** – A structured post-incident activity used to evaluate the effectiveness of the incident response, identify root causes, and implement corrective actions.
