# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Enterprise Threat Intelligence Policy is to establish governance requirements for collecting, analyzing, sharing, and applying cyber threat intelligence to improve the organization's ability to identify, prevent, detect, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity threats.

This policy establishes a standardized Enterprise Threat Intelligence (CTI) framework that enables informed risk-based decision-making, strengthens proactive defense capabilities, supports incident response, and improves organizational cyber resilience.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All employees.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Temporary workers.
- Third-party vendors.
- Managed service providers.
- Cloud environments.
- On-premises environments.
- Hybrid environments.
- Security Operations Center (SOC).
- Threat Intelligence Team.
- Incident Response Team.
- Information Security Team.
- Vulnerability Management Team.
- Enterprise technology assets.
- Business-critical information systems.

This policy applies to all cyber threat intelligence activities supporting the protection of enterprise information assets.

---

# 3. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Establish enterprise threat intelligence governance.
- Improve proactive cyber defense.
- Enhance threat detection capabilities.
- Support risk-based decision-making.
- Strengthen incident response.
- Improve vulnerability prioritization.
- Support threat hunting activities.
- Facilitate secure intelligence sharing.
- Improve organizational cyber resilience.

---

# 4. Policy Statement

The organization shall maintain a formal Enterprise Threat Intelligence Program to collect, analyze, produce, share, and operationalize cyber threat intelligence.

Threat intelligence activities shall be risk-based, documented, continuously monitored, and integrated with Security Operations, Incident Management, Vulnerability Management, Risk Management, and Security Architecture functions.

Threat intelligence shall be used to improve security monitoring, prioritize defensive actions, strengthen detection capabilities, support executive decision-making, and reduce organizational cyber risk.

---

# 5. Threat Intelligence Governance

The organization shall maintain a formal Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) governance framework to oversee the collection, analysis, production, dissemination, and operational use of cyber threat intelligence.

Threat intelligence governance shall include:

- Executive sponsorship.
- Defined governance roles and responsibilities.
- Enterprise threat intelligence standards.
- Risk-based decision-making.
- Intelligence quality assurance.
- Regulatory compliance oversight.
- Performance monitoring.
- Periodic governance reviews.
- Cross-functional coordination.
- Continuous improvement initiatives.

Threat intelligence governance shall align with Enterprise Risk Management, Security Operations Center (SOC), Incident Management, and Vulnerability Management governance.

---

# 6. Threat Intelligence Sources

Threat intelligence shall be collected from trusted internal and external sources.

Approved intelligence sources include:

- Internal security events.
- Security Operations Center (SOC) findings.
- Incident response investigations.
- Vulnerability assessments.
- Penetration testing results.
- Commercial threat intelligence providers.
- Government advisories.
- Industry Information Sharing and Analysis Centers (ISACs).
- Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT).
- Vendor security advisories.
- Security researchers.
- Trusted partner intelligence sharing.

Threat intelligence sources shall be periodically evaluated for relevance, accuracy, and reliability.

---

# 7. Threat Intelligence Collection

Threat intelligence shall be collected using documented procedures and approved technologies.

Collection activities include:

- Threat indicator collection.
- Malware intelligence.
- Adversary behavior monitoring.
- Vulnerability intelligence.
- Dark web monitoring where appropriate.
- Threat actor tracking.
- Campaign monitoring.
- Attack technique collection.
- Emerging threat identification.
- Continuous intelligence gathering

---

# 8. Threat Intelligence Analysis

Collected threat intelligence shall be analyzed to determine relevance, credibility, impact, and recommended defensive actions.

Threat intelligence analysis shall include:

- Threat actor profiling.
- Campaign analysis.
- Malware analysis.
- Indicator of Compromise (IOC) analysis.
- Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTP) analysis.
- MITRE ATT&CK mapping.
- Risk assessment.
- Impact analysis.
- Confidence scoring.
- Intelligence validation.

Threat intelligence products shall be reviewed for accuracy and operational value before distribution.

---

# 9. Intelligence Lifecycle

The organization shall manage cyber threat intelligence throughout its lifecycle.

The intelligence lifecycle shall include:

- Planning and direction.
- Collection.
- Processing.
- Analysis.
- Production.
- Dissemination.
- Operational use.
- Feedback.
- Continuous improvement.
- Periodic review.

Threat intelligence activities shall follow documented procedures to ensure consistency and effectiveness.

---

# 10. Threat Intelligence Sharing

Threat intelligence shall be shared with authorized internal and external stakeholders when appropriate.

Threat intelligence sharing requirements include:

- Need-to-know principles.
- Executive approval where required.
- Information classification compliance.
- Secure communication channels.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Third-party agreements.
- Intelligence source protection.
- Traffic Light Protocol (TLP) labeling where applicable.
- Documentation of intelligence sharing.
- Periodic review of sharing practices.

Threat intelligence sharing shall support collaborative defense while protecting sensitive organizational information.

---

# 11. Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)

The organization shall collect, validate, manage, and operationalize Indicators of Compromise (IOCs) to improve threat detection and response.

IOC management activities include:

- Collection of internal IOCs.
- Collection of external IOCs.
- IOC validation.
- IOC enrichment.
- IOC prioritization.
- IOC distribution.
- IOC lifecycle management.
- IOC retirement.
- SIEM integration.
- Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) integration.

IOCs shall be reviewed periodically to ensure continued relevance and effectiveness.

---

# 12. Threat Hunting

The organization shall conduct proactive threat hunting activities to identify malicious activity that may evade automated detection.

Threat hunting activities include:

- Hypothesis-driven investigations.
- IOC-based hunting.
- MITRE ATT&CK technique hunting.
- Endpoint telemetry analysis.
- Network traffic analysis.
- Cloud environment analysis.
- Identity activity analysis.
- Privileged account monitoring.
- Threat hunting documentation.
- Lessons learned reviews.

Threat hunting results shall be documented and integrated into detection engineering and incident response processes.

---

# 13. Threat Intelligence Platform (TIP)

Where implemented, the organization shall use an approved Threat Intelligence Platform (TIP) to manage cyber threat intelligence.

TIP capabilities shall include:

- Intelligence aggregation.
- IOC management.
- Threat feed integration.
- Intelligence enrichment.
- Automated distribution.
- Intelligence search.
- Integration with SIEM.
- Integration with SOAR platforms where applicable.
- Reporting and dashboards.
- Audit logging.

Threat Intelligence Platforms shall be configured according to enterprise security standards.

---

# 14. Third-Party Threat Intelligence

Third-party cyber threat intelligence providers shall be evaluated and approved through the enterprise vendor management process.

Third-party intelligence requirements include:

- Vendor due diligence.
- Source reliability assessment.
- Contractual security obligations.
- Information sharing agreements.
- Data quality validation.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Protection of shared information.
- Periodic vendor performance reviews.
- Audit rights where applicable.
- Continuous monitoring.

Third-party threat intelligence shall align with the Enterprise Third-Party Security Policy.

---

# 15. Metrics and Reporting

The organization shall maintain metrics to evaluate the effectiveness of the Enterprise Threat Intelligence Program.

Metrics may include:

- Intelligence reports produced.
- IOC ingestion rates.
- IOC detection rates.
- Threat hunting activities completed.
- Threat intelligence sharing activities.
- Intelligence source performance.
- Incident response support metrics.
- Mean Time to Detect (MTTD).
- Mean Time to Respond (MTTR).
- Executive reporting.

Threat intelligence metrics shall be reviewed regularly to identify improvement opportunities.

# 16. Roles and Responsibilities

## Executive Management

Executive Management shall:

- Approve the Enterprise Threat Intelligence Program.
- Allocate appropriate resources.
- Review enterprise cyber threat risks.
- Support continuous improvement initiatives.

## Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

The CISO shall:

- Own this policy.
- Oversee enterprise threat intelligence governance.
- Approve intelligence sharing strategies.
- Review intelligence metrics.
- Report significant cyber threat trends to Executive Management.

## Threat Intelligence Team

The Threat Intelligence Team shall:

- Collect and analyze cyber threat intelligence.
- Produce intelligence reports.
- Manage Indicators of Compromise (IOCs).
- Support threat hunting.
- Coordinate intelligence sharing.
- Recommend defensive actions.

## Security Operations Center (SOC)

The Security Operations Center (SOC) shall:

- Operationalize threat intelligence.
- Monitor intelligence-driven alerts.
- Investigate IOC matches.
- Escalate significant threats.
- Support incident response.
- Maintain SIEM detection content.

## Information Security Team

The Information Security Team shall:

- Integrate threat intelligence into security controls.
- Support risk assessments.
- Coordinate with vulnerability management.
- Review intelligence effectiveness.
- Recommend security improvements.

## Employees

Employees shall:

- Report suspected phishing and suspicious activity.
- Protect sensitive threat intelligence.
- Participate in security awareness training.
- Follow enterprise incident reporting procedures.
- Support organizational cyber defense initiatives.

# 22. Definitions

**Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI)** – Evidence-based knowledge about existing or emerging cyber threats, including context, indicators, adversary capabilities, infrastructure, and recommended defensive actions.

**Indicator of Compromise (IOC)** – A forensic artifact or observable that indicates a system may have been compromised, such as a malicious IP address, domain, file hash, or registry key.

**Threat Hunting** – A proactive security activity that searches enterprise environments for malicious activity that has evaded automated detection mechanisms.

**Threat Intelligence Platform (TIP)** – A platform used to collect, aggregate, enrich, manage, analyze, and distribute cyber threat intelligence.

**MITRE ATT&CK** – A globally recognized knowledge base of adversary tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) based on real-world observations.

**Traffic Light Protocol (TLP)** – A standardized information-sharing protocol used to indicate how sensitive threat intelligence may be shared.
